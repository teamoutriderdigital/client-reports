Google is fundamentally changing the rules of local search. If your local SEO strategy looks the same as it did in 2024, you are already falling behind. The organic Google Local Pack is shrinking, AI is stealing traffic, fake reviews are turning into blackmail, and visibility is rapidly shifting to a “pay-to-play” model.

01

The Evolving SERP Landscape: AI and Ads Take Over

Even businesses ranking #1 organically are seeing a steep drop in call volume. This isn’t a tracking error; it is a deliberate redesign of the Search Engine Results Page (SERP) by Google to prioritize paid and AI-driven real estate.

The Rise of AI Local Packs

Google is aggressively testing AI-powered local packs. Here is exactly how they function:

  • Current Rollout: They are currently only appearing on mobile devices in the US. However, they are showing up on roughly 8% of all local queries (up from 7% just weeks prior), affecting industries across the board: lawyers, electricians, dentists, and elder care.
  • The UI/UX Nightmare: These AI packs replace the traditional 3-pack. Instead of showing three businesses, they typically show 1.5 businesses (one full listing and half of a second one), requiring users to click “more” to see others.
  • Friction for Users: The AI packs remove the giant “Call” button. If a user clicks the white space, it takes them to the Google Business Profile (GBP). To get to the website, users have to click a microscopic link icon that is incredibly difficult to tap on a mobile screen.
  • Volatile Ranking Patterns: Unlike traditional local packs that show consistent grid patterns (e.g., green in the center, fading to yellow and red on the outskirts), AI pack rankings are highly volatile. A business might show up 30% of the time on one scan, and 30% on the next, but in completely different geographic nodes.

The Invasion of Local Pack Ads

Traditional Google Ads appearing inside the organic Local Pack have skyrocketed.

  • The Data: In January, ads appeared in the local pack about 1% of the time. By November, that number jumped to 14% to 17% of the time.
  • Visual Camouflage: These ads are incredibly well-hidden. They look exactly like organic listings, tricking even seasoned marketers into clicking them.
  • The “Local Finder” Ad Squeeze: If a user clicks “More Businesses” to view the Local Finder, they are now frequently greeted by three sponsored ads at the very top before seeing a single organic result.

Local Services Ads (LSAs) Dominate

It is now common to see a SERP that looks like this: Three Local Services Ads at the top, followed by a traditional search ad, followed by a Local Pack ad, and finally the organic Local Pack. The organic results have been pushed entirely below the fold.

02

Mastering Paid Acquisition: LSAs and Traditional Ads

Because Google is squeezing organic real estate, local businesses must embrace paid advertising. However, you have to know how to play the game.

Winning with Local Services Ads (LSAs)

LSAs are incredibly lucrative for high-ticket industries (like Personal Injury Law or Roofers) but can be a tough pill to swallow for low-ticket services (like appliance repair) where leads cost $30–$50 each.

  • Budget is #1: If you aren’t bidding enough, you won’t be seen. Google prioritizes businesses willing to pay for the click.
  • Review Recency: Your LSA profile is tied directly to your GBP reviews. A steady influx of recent reviews is one of the strongest ranking factors for LSAs.
  • Call Handling and AI Sentiment: Google routes LSA calls through their system. Their AI listens to, transcribes, and analyzes the sentiment of your calls. Crucial Tip: If you get an LSA lead for a small job you don’t want (e.g., repairing a single shingle), do not reject it on the phone. Google’s AI may flag you as a business that rejects leads, tanking your LSA visibility. Instead, say, “Let me check with my manager and call you right back,” take their information, hang up, and follow up later to decline the job.
  • Profile Settings: Turn off broad categories like “General Law” in your settings, or you will pay for junk leads.

Tracking Local Pack Ads

If you run traditional Google Ads that appear in the Local Pack, tracking them is notoriously difficult.

  • The Hidden Setting: Go to your GBP settings, click the three dots, select “Advanced,” and look for the “Google Ads Phone Number” field. Put a call-tracking number (like CallRail) in this specific field. This is the only way to accurately track how many calls your Local Pack ads are actually generating.
03

Google Business Profile (GBP) Advanced Tactics & Nuances

How you set up your GBP has massive implications for your visibility.

The “Openness” Factor

Google gives a definitive ranking boost to businesses that are currently open.

  • The Two-Stage Drop: Rankings don’t just disappear when you close. An hour before closing, your profile displays “Closing Soon,” which causes a slight drop in visibility. Once you are fully closed, your visibility drops significantly as open competitors overtake you.
  • The Strategy: If you have a physical office, consider extending your stated hours by opening an hour earlier or closing an hour later. If you are a service business, consider listing your hours as 24/7 and using a third-party answering service to capture leads from 10 PM to 6 AM.

The Service Area Business (SAB) Penalty

If you hide your address on your GBP (making you an SAB), you are at a proven ranking disadvantage.

  • The Maps Deficit: SABs do not get a map pin, and they cannot receive driving directions (a massive engagement signal for Google). If you look at GBP Insights, a storefront might get 30% of its visibility from Maps, while an SAB will often see 1% or less.
  • The “Moving” Bug: You cannot change the address of a Service Area Business. If you try, the profile will either revert to the original verification address, or you may fall victim to a known Google bug that teleports your ranking radius 200 miles away into the middle of a desert. If you move cities, you must create a brand new GBP.
  • The Loophole: If you run a business from your home, try to legally show your address if privacy allows. Joy Hawkins shared a case study of an at-home beauty salon that was suspended, but successfully reinstated because they had permanent, professional interior signage in their basement salon. Alternatively, sublease a small, legitimate office room (not a co-working space) to get a real address.
  • Service Area Suspensions: Do not set up multiple GBPs with overlapping service areas. This cannibalizes your rankings and is a massive, almost guaranteed suspension risk. (Note: The “2-hour driving radius” guideline is rarely enforced as a suspension trigger, but overlapping territories absolutely are).
04

The Dark Side of Reviews: Extortion, Jail, and Generation

Reviews are your lifeblood, but Google’s policing of them is broken, and scammers are taking advantage.

Review Extortion

Overseas scammers are targeting local businesses by leaving strings of highly detailed, 1-star fake reviews.

  • The WhatsApp Trigger: Scammers specifically target businesses that have added a WhatsApp number to their GBP, as it allows for untraceable extortion. They will message the business demanding thousands of dollars to remove the reviews.
  • The Solution: Do not pay. Google has launched a dedicated “Merchant Extortion” reporting form. If you use this form, Google is currently doing an excellent job of removing the fake reviews within 24 to 48 hours.

“Review Jail”

Google is now slapping 30-day restrictions on profiles, deleting reviews, and blocking new ones for a month.

  • The Triggers: This used to only happen if a customer reported you for offering incentives (e.g., a free coffee for a review). Now, Google is triggering this based on sustained volume spikes. If your review volume spikes by 300% and stays there for 90 days, Google assumes you are buying reviews or incentivizing them.
  • Foot Traffic Theory: Experts theorize Google compares your review volume to GPS foot traffic data (based on mobile phones entering your store). If 90% of your daily foot traffic leaves a review, Google’s algorithm flags it as unnatural.

Generation and Management

  • SMS over Email: Do not use email to ask for reviews (25% open rate). Use SMS text messages with a direct link (98% open rate).
  • Staff Incentives: Run internal contests. Offer a $50 or $100 bonus to the employee who gets the most client reviews in a month.
  • Third-Party Fake Reviews: If someone leaves a negative review on behalf of someone else (e.g., “My friend was treated poorly here”), Google will usually remove it. However, if they don’t explicitly state it was third-party, Google will never remove it, as you cannot prove the reviewer wasn’t a real customer. Just reply professionally and bury it with 5-star reviews.
05

Diversification: Life Beyond Google

With Google’s organic leads shrinking, you must diversify.

Video Marketing (YouTube & TikTok)

Video is the ultimate trust-builder and is immune to AI text generation.

  • Authenticity Wins: You don’t need high production value. Luke Durand shared a case study of a UK electrician who films himself repairing niche “underfloor heating systems” and “storage heaters” with his smartphone. By showing exactly how he works (e.g., proving he won’t destroy the client’s flooring), he builds instant rapport and generates highly qualified leads directly from YouTube.
  • TikTok Ads: Darren Shaw tested boosting a TikTok video for $70 CAD and received 27,000 views, 585 likes, and 12 comments. While not traditionally used for local services, the underpriced attention on social video is worth experimenting with.

Optimizing for AI (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity)

While AI chatbots currently drive only a tiny fraction of traffic compared to Google (around 2% for major sites), it is growing.

  • Entity SEO: You don’t need a crazy new strategy for this. Focus on writing in clear “semantic triples” on your website (e.g., “Bob’s Plumbing provides water heater repair in Dallas, Texas”). Repeat your business name and services clearly so AI models understand exactly who you are.
  • Best-of Lists: LLMs pull heavily from directories and articles. Try to get your business listed on local “Best of [City]” lists.
  • Beware of Snake Oil: Do not buy SEO software that claims to track your “LLM Visibility” on a grid. Because AI outputs are highly randomized, this data is entirely fabricated and useless.
06

Black Hat Tactics & What to STOP Doing in 2026

Stop wasting your budget on SEO myths, outdated practices, and risky manipulation.

Risky/Black Hat Tactics

  • CTR (Click-Through Rate) Manipulation: Using bots or paid clickers to search your brand and click your listing does work, but it is incredibly risky. Furthermore, it only works if you are already ranking in the top 5 to 20 spots. If you are ranking #50, it won’t help you.
  • Foot Traffic Spoofing: Some black hats physically carry 100 mobile phones in a backpack into a store to spoof GPS foot traffic and boost rankings. While it theoretically works, the logistical nightmare and risk of a permanent ban make it a terrible strategy for legitimate businesses.

Dead Tactics to Abandon Immediately

  1. Geo-Tagging Images

    Adding coordinates to the EXIF data of your images does absolutely nothing for your rankings. It is the #1 local SEO myth.

  2. Mass Citation Building

    Submitting your business to hundreds of low-quality directories is a waste of time. Stick to the core, high-authority sites: Yelp, Better Business Bureau, Apple Maps, MapQuest, and industry-specific directories. (Tip: Check the domain authority of a directory in Ahrefs before bothering to build a citation there).

  3. Generic Content Creation

    Stop paying agencies to write generic blog posts like “5 Spring Cleaning Tips.” AI can write that in 3 seconds, and nobody reads it.

  4. Local Business Schema

    Darren Shaw advises against obsessing over local schema. It provides almost no ranking value and often causes NAP (Name, Address, Phone) consistency issues if you update your website but forget to update the hidden schema code.

  5. Exact-Match Anchor Text Links

    Buying guest posts with heavy, keyword-rich anchor text (e.g., linking the words “Dallas Plumber” to your site) is now actively penalized by Google’s spam updates.

07

The 2026 Action Plan: What Actually Works

To win in 2026, implement these core strategies:

  1. Content Freshness

    Don’t just build a service page and leave it to rot. Regularly update your existing pages, add new FAQs, and refresh the content. Google rewards active maintenance with noticeable ranking boosts.

  2. Client Interviews for Content

    Because generic content is dead, SEOs should interview business owners to extract unique stories, recent trends, and real-world case studies that AI cannot replicate.

  3. Track Leads, Not Rankings

    Because of AI packs, LSAs, and personalized search, tracking organic rankings is becoming convoluted. Shift your focus entirely to tracking leads and conversions. Use CallRail, and train your staff to ask every single caller: “How did you hear about us?”

  4. Flawless Fulfillment

    Don’t waste money ranking for high-volume keywords if your customer service is poor. Answering the phone quickly, responding to quote requests immediately, and replying to reviews professionally will drive the word-of-mouth and retention required to survive in a pay-to-play ecosystem.
